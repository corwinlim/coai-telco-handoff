## Audience selection
- Person A: frontline connectivity / broadband / mobile support representative
- Person B: field technician coordinator or dispatch representative
- Person C: team leader, QA or operations manager responsible for ticket-hand-off quality

Begin with the **problem**, not a pitch. Ideally speak to people who handle complaints and technician handoffs in daily work. Record only professional role, non-sensitive workflow examples and voluntary consent.

## English invitation — direct message / email
Subject: 15–20 minute research chat about telecom support handoffs

Hello,

I'm researching how connectivity support teams pass incident information from first-line support to technicians. I have a small prototype called CoAi Telco Handoff and would like to understand existing handoff challenges before considering any pilot.

Would you be open to a 15–20 minute conversation about your general process and what currently works or fails? I'm not asking for customer records, confidential screenshots, account details, or access to your systems. If useful, I can show a fictional test case, and you're free to critique or reject the idea.

This is exploratory product research, not a sales commitment or a request for endorsement. Participation is voluntary, and I would ask permission before recording or attributing any quote.

Thank you.

## Ethical and evidence controls
1. Do not use customer identifiers, incident tickets or live operational screenshots.
2. Do not claim an interview happened unless it did. Differentiate sent, replied, booked and completed.
3. Ask permission for recording and for attributing quotations; avoid storing more personal data than needed.
4. If a prospect declines, record only the opt-out status and do not repeatedly contact them.
5. Keep the demo marked SYNTHETIC; do not suggest real network or carrier integration.
6. Offer the prototype only as an optional fictional example, not as a tested professional tool.

## Decision gate
After 3 **genuine** interviews, assess: (i) recurring missed context, (ii) incumbent CRM workflow adequacy, (iii) duplicate entry costs, (iv) a measurable outcome, and (v) an authorized pilot sponsor. Two of three descriptions of material pain are necessary but insufficient without a feasible safe pilot route.
